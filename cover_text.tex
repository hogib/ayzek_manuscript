Dear Editors-in-Chief,

\medskip
Please find enclosed the manuscript ``A Lightweight Detection Pipeline for Earthquake
Early Warning and Its Evaluation on Three Seismic Networks'', which we
submit for exclusive consideration as a research article in Computers \&
Geosciences. We confirm that the submission follows the journal's requirements and
includes every item of its submission checklist.

\medskip
The manuscript addresses both of the journal's scopes. Computationally, it presents
ayzek, a self-contained pipeline for the detection stage of earthquake early
warning that also reports a P-wave displacement magnitude. Its
hand-written inference reproduces the PyTorch networks to within floating-point
rounding, its replays of archived data are deterministic, and the same build runs on a
Raspberry Pi~4. It also presents a replay protocol, implemented as a reproducible
workflow, that measures detectors at their operating point with chance correction,
paired tests and catalog-quiet data, whose settings, including the baselines', were
fixed in a public, timestamped release before any test waveform was fetched. Geoscientifically, it measures how a learned detector, two classical
triggers and two published models behave on continuous records of three networks
under that protocol: the Greek networks served by the National Observatory of
Athens and New Zealand's GeoNet, which the learned detector had not seen, and the Turkish KO network
the detector was trained on, held out in time. At the settings matched on unmatched
alarms the learned detector detected 22.4 more chance-corrected events than STA/LTA of
177 on the Greek sequences, though with twice its unmatched alarms, and 18.1 more of
151 on the New Zealand sequences, with equal unmatched alarms; the KO test, under its
target in its two-month pool, was not significant. Causal PhaseNet was equivalent to
the learned detector on both unseen networks, so the gain is that of learned detectors
over an amplitude trigger rather than of ours. On the catalog-quiet windows the learned
detector's alarm rate was not distinguishable from STA/LTA's on New Zealand or Greece,
where an exploratory analysis traces half of its alarms to one station's emergent
noise that STA/LTA ignores, and its alarms came 1.2 to 2.9 s later. On a 728-day
record at one station, two detectors with nearly equal ROC-AUC differed by a factor of 1.6 in
recall at one false alarm per day.

\medskip
The source code, model weights, the protocol and the test settings, and the reproduction
scripts are in a public repository under the GNU General Public License, archived on
Zenodo, with the details listed in the section ``Code availability''. The test
waveforms are open at their data centers and are not redistributed; the released
request records and file hashes let anyone fetch and check the same data. The AFAD
development waveforms cannot be redistributed, and public KOERI datasets reproduce the
development results.
